The loop, walked once more

Walk it once, slowly. A prompt arrives as text and is cut into pieces — the frequency-driven cuts of the tokenizer. Each piece becomes a row in a matrix: an embedding vector, rotated by its position, so that attention can tell pieces apart by where they sit.

Then the layer stack. In every layer, each vector asks questions of the vectors before it — queries against cached keys, weighted sums of values — gathering context; then, alone, it passes through the feed-forward net, the layer's private store of facts and patterns. The stack's output is one vector: the final position's prediction, flattened by a softmax into a probability for every piece in the vocabulary.

The sampler draws one piece — greedy, or tempered, or nucleus-cut. The drawn piece is appended to the sequence, and everything runs again: one more position through every layer, one more row in every layer's cache, one more distribution. That is the whole machine: a loop that turns a probability distribution into text one draw at a time, with every earlier page a study of one box or one cost inside it.

What the loop cannot do is remember. Between requests the machine keeps nothing: a conversation exists only as the pieces currently inside the window — which is why the request page's compounding prompt is the whole of a chat's memory, and why the window page's stretching tricks matter at all. The loop is the machine's entire notion of time.

The costs gave the parts their shape. Part One built the boxes and showed why each exists: embeddings to carry meaning, rotations to carry order, attention to gather, feed-forward nets to store. Part Two: the loop is only as good as the training that shaped its distributions. Part Three taught the loop its manners — post-training turned an autocompleter into an assistant, and the sampler turned one answer into many. Part Four: the loop's floor — one sweep of the weights per piece — and the five ways around it. Part Five: the window's edge, and the request that threads it all.

More capacity without more arithmetic

The dense model has one awkward property: every piece pays for every weight, and the feed-forward nets are the bulk of those weights. A mixture-of-experts design breaks the bulk into parts: many separate experts — feed-forward nets — plus a small router that reads each vector and consults only a few of them per piece [state-2026].

The decoupling is the point: total capacity — stored knowledge — lives in all the experts together, but per-piece arithmetic tracks only the consulted few. Mixtral proved the recipe in open weights: under thirteen billion active weights matching or beating a dense seventy-billion model — five times fewer active parameters for the same quality [state-2026].

The frontier runs on it. DeepSeek's third generation: six hundred seventy-one billion total, thirty-seven billion active, two hundred fifty-six routed experts plus one shared, eight consulted per piece. Llama's fourth: the same shape at two sizes, both with seventeen billion active [state-2026].

The training bill tells the same story: the whole six-hundred-seventy-one-billion-weight model was trained on nearly fifteen trillion pieces of text for about two point eight million accelerator-hours [state-2026].

The catch is the one Part Four taught: inference cost tracks active weights, but memory holds total weights. A mixture trades bandwidth for capacity — memory, not arithmetic, becomes the binding constraint [state-2026].

The cache, compressed again

The KV cache got its own page and its own bill; the toolbox keeps shrinking it. Grouped-query attention — many query heads sharing a handful of key-value heads — was the first cut. MLA goes further: compress each piece's keys and values into a small latent vector, cache only that, and let the heads reconstruct what they need from it — an even smaller cache at the same reach [state-2026].

Around it, the rest of the toolbox ships: windowed layers next to full-attention ones, layers with no positional information at all, multi-piece prediction heads that double as draft proposals for speculation [state-2026].

The third axis: thinking

Everything so far scaled two axes: more parameters, more data. The newest axis spends at run time: test-time compute — more compute per query, after training, at inference [state-2026].

The mechanism is disarmingly familiar: a chain of thought — the model generating intermediate steps before the answer — is more decode steps. A long think is not a bigger model; it is more sampled pieces feeding back as context, each pass paying the same bandwidth floor Part Four priced. One hard query can run tens of thousands of forward passes [state-2026].

How did models learn to think? Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards — RLVR: rewards a program can check — mathematics that verifies, code that runs — rather than a human's preference. Train against that signal and the long think emerges; the raw chain stays hidden, summarized for the user instead. The post-training stack converged with it: supervised fine-tuning, preference optimization, and verifiable-reward reinforcement — the recipes of the major closed and open lines alike [state-2026].

The arrival proof: o1, the first of the class, scored eighty-three percent on an olympiad qualifying exam where its sibling GPT-4o scored thirteen; DeepSeek-R1 matched the recipe in open weights months later; and the largest thinking models expose a thinking budget — a dial for how much inference compute one query may spend [state-2026].

The bill follows the machinery. A thinking model is the bandwidth floor's best customer: tens of thousands of decode steps per query means tens of thousands of weight sweeps, and a fleet sized for short chatty replies must either queue the long thinks or cap them. The thinking budget is that economics surfaced as a setting: the same dial, pointed at the user.

What remains open

An honest close needs the open ledger. Is the chain of thought faithful — does the text of the reasoning reflect the computation, or is it a plausible story beside it? The field does not know; treat shown reasoning as evidence, not confession [state-2026].

Hallucination is structural, not a bug to patch next release: the sampler draws from a distribution, and nothing in the machinery checks the draw against the world. Mitigations — retrieval, verification, tools — route around it; none cures it [state-2026].

Long-context claims outrun effective use — the middle of a giant window is read worse than its ends. And reasoning distills imperfectly: the long think of a big model compresses into a small one, but something is always lost [state-2026].

The whole machine, in one paragraph

Here is the machine, whole. A tokenizer cuts text into pieces; each piece becomes a vector and is rotated by its position; layers of attention — each vector gathering context from the cached keys and values behind it — and feed-forward transforms — each vector processed alone — refine the row; a softmax turns the final vector into a distribution over every piece; a sampler draws one; the draw is appended and the loop runs again. Training shaped those distributions with next-piece prediction on a vast corpus, and post-training taught them the shape of a helpful turn. Serving runs the loop under a bandwidth floor, with caches and batches and small drafts and small numbers bending it. That is the whole of it — one loop, one distribution at a time.

Takeaways

  • The whole machine is one loop: tokenize, embed and rotate, attend over the cache, transform, score, sample, append, repeat — every page of this book studied one box or one cost inside it.
  • Mixture-of-experts decouples capacity from arithmetic: many feed-forward experts, a few consulted per piece (Mixtral: 12.9B active matching a 70B dense; DeepSeek-V3: 37B of 671B) — and the binding constraint moves to memory.
  • The attention toolbox ships compressed caches — GQA, then MLA's latent compression — beside windowed and position-free layers.
  • Test-time compute is the third scaling axis: a chain of thought is more decode steps, not a bigger model — trained by RL with verifiable rewards, spendable through a thinking budget (o1: 83% vs 13% on an olympiad qualifying exam).
  • What stays open: chain-of-thought faithfulness, structural hallucination, the long-context middle, and what distillation loses.
