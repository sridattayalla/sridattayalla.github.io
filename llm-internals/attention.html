One pronoun, one problem

The last page ended with every vector carrying its slot as geometry. The pieces still cannot see each other. Each hidden state is a sealed column — one per piece — and from the ground floor to the top, no information has yet moved between positions.

Here is a sentence that needs movement: "the cat ate it". Four pieces, and the fourth is in trouble. Whatever comes next — "purred", say, which is a cat thing to do — depends on what "it" refers to, and the useful information sits in "cat", three slots back, sealed inside another vector [attention-example].

So before the floors can predict anything, something must move cat-ness into the it-position. That something is a gather: every piece builds a new vector as a mixture drawn from the other pieces, weighted by relevance. This page builds the gather from scratch, with numbers small enough to check by hand.

Three copies of every vector

The gather needs every piece to play three roles at once — what it wants, what it offers, what it gives — so the machinery makes three copies of every hidden state, each pushed through its own learned table. The query is the asking copy, what this piece is looking for in the others, produced by the hidden state times a learned table W_q. The key is the offering copy, the label a piece wears to be found by (W_k). The value is the payload, what the piece actually hands over when selected (W_v).

Mechanically there is nothing new: three matrix multiplies, three learned tables, the front-door operation from two pages back in three different roles. The names come from database talk — query against keys, receive values — and the tables are learned, so each model decides for itself what asking and offering mean. Everything that follows is plumbing.

Scores, weights, and the gather

The comparison is brute force. Every piece's query is dotted with every piece's key: four pieces give sixteen raw scores, a square grid with rows asking and columns answering. Each score is then divided by the square root of the comparison width, for a reason worth a beat: with wide vectors, dot products come out large, and a softmax fed large numbers goes flat — one entry swallows the distribution and the rest of the comparison carries no information. The division keeps the scores where the softmax stays discriminating [attention-example].

Note

Where the last page plugs in: right before these dot products, the rotation is applied to the asking and offering copies, so every score automatically feels the gap between the two slots. The hand-checkable numbers below skip the rotation to stay small; the companion script's full model applies it exactly as shipped.

A softmax down each row then turns the sixteen scores into sixteen fractions between zero and one, each row summing to one. The whole apparatus — the three copies, the comparison grid, the weighting, the mixture that follows — is the mechanism the architecture is named for: attention, and pieces are said to attend to each other. Its bookkeeping is the grid of row fractions, the attention weights: weight (i, j) is the share of piece i's gather that comes from piece j.

The mixture itself — the gather — is a weighted sum: piece i's new vector is every piece's value added up, each scaled by its weight in row i. One grid times one stack of values: multiply and add, like everything else in this machine.

Nobody sees the future

One edit to the grid before the softmax: any score whose column lies strictly after its row is blocked, set to a number so negative that the softmax renders it exactly zero. This is the causal mask, and it makes the grid triangular — the upper-right half is dead, always exactly zero.

The reason is training, not taste. The model learns by predicting, at every slot in the same pass, the piece that comes next; a slot that could gather from later slots would be gathering the answer it is meant to guess. Blocking the future is what makes the exercise honest at every position at once.

The whole computation, by hand

Now the full mechanism on "the cat ate it", width two, with hand-picked numbers. Every matrix below is printed by the companion script's second stage and recorded in the research sheet; nothing is rounded past three decimals [attention-example].

Worked example — the sentence and its three copies

sentence: "the cat ate it"        4 pieces, width 2

hand-picked embedding rows E:          asking table:
            dim0  dim1                 W_q = [ 2  0 ]
   the       0     1                        [ 0  1 ]
   cat       2     0                 (W_k = W_v = identity)
   ate       1     1
   it        1     0

the three copies (each row = one piece's copy):
   Q = E @ W_q           K = E                V = E
   the [ 0  1 ]           the [ 0  1 ]          the [ 0  1 ]
   cat [ 4  0 ]           cat [ 2  0 ]          cat [ 2  0 ]
   ate [ 2  1 ]           ate [ 1  1 ]          ate [ 1  1 ]
   it  [ 2  0 ]           it  [ 1  0 ]          it  [ 1  0 ]

The asking table here simply doubles the first entry of every row — chosen so that the pronoun's asking copy leans hard on the first entry, which happens to be where "cat" carries its weight.

Worked example — scores, before the mask

every query dotted with every key, divided by sqrt(2):

   the pronoun's raw scores, before scaling:
      q_it . k_the = 2*0 + 0*1 = 0
      q_it . k_cat = 2*2 + 0*0 = 4
      q_it . k_ate = 2*1 + 0*1 = 2
      q_it . k_it  = 2*1 + 0*0 = 2

   S = Q @ K^T / sqrt(2)   (rows ask, columns answer):
    the [ 0.707  0.      0.707  0.   ]
    cat [ 0.     5.657   2.828  2.828]
    ate [ 0.707  2.828   2.121  1.414]
    it  [ 0.     2.828   1.414  1.414]

Read the pronoun's row before the mask: the raw agreement of its asking copy is zero with "the", four with "cat", two with "ate", two with itself. The grid above shows those already divided by the square root of the width.

Worked example — the mask and the row softmax

block the future (upper triangle), then softmax down each row:

   the pronoun's row, by hand:
      scores      [ 0.      2.828   1.414   1.414 ]
      minus max   [-2.828   0.     -1.414  -1.414 ]
      exp          [ 0.059   1.      0.243   0.243 ]
      / sum 1.545  [ 0.038   0.647   0.157   0.157 ]

   A = softmax(masked S), all rows:
    the [ 1.     0.     0.     0.   ]
    cat [ 0.003  0.997  0.     0.   ]
    ate [ 0.074  0.620  0.306  0.   ]
    it  [ 0.038  0.647  0.157  0.157]

The pronoun's row, done by hand: subtract the row's largest score, exponentiate, divide by the sum. About two thirds of its gather now comes from "cat". The first row is degenerate on purpose — "the" can see only itself, so its softmax is a certainty, all weight on itself.

Worked example — the gather

out = A @ V   (each row of out = weighted sum of the value rows)

   the pronoun's gather, spelled out:
      0.038*[0 1] + 0.647*[2 0] + 0.157*[1 1] + 0.157*[1 0]
      = [ 1.609  0.196 ]

   all outputs:
    the [ 0.      1.   ]
    cat [ 1.993   0.003]
    ate [ 1.546   0.380]
    it  [ 1.609   0.196 ]

And the gather: the pronoun's new vector is dominated by the cat-piece's payload. Its input was a copy of "it"; its output leans toward "cat". Whatever the higher floors make of the sentence, the it-position now holds cat-ness to work with — which is what predicting "purred" needs [attention-example].

One full copy of this apparatus — three tables, comparison, gather — is called a head. A model could run exactly one head per floor and it would work. Real models run many, which is the next page's subject.

What looking costs

The grid is the price. Every query meets every key: with n pieces the grid holds n-times-n scores, and both the work and the memory grow with the square of the piece count — quadratic in length, linear in width [attention-example].

That square is why the model's reach is finite and fixed at build time. The context window is the number of pieces the model can hold at once — everything outside it simply does not exist for the machine. GPT-3's window is 2,048 pieces [model-configs]; at that size, one weight grid per head per floor holds about four million scores, and models stack dozens of heads across dozens of floors. Keeping that affordable is the next page's second subject.

Takeaways

  • Every piece makes three copies of its hidden state through three learned tables: a query (what to look for), a key (what to be found by), a value (what to hand over).
  • Scores are every query dotted with every key, scaled down to keep the softmax discriminating; a softmax down each row turns them into attention weights.
  • The causal mask blocks every forward-looking score, so training can demand a prediction at every slot in one pass; the grid is triangular.
  • In the toy sentence the pronoun drew about two thirds of its gather from "cat" — reference-finding out of dot products and a softmax.
  • One copy of the apparatus is a head; the score grid grows with the square of the piece count, which is why the context window is finite. Next: many heads per floor.
