# FS-PATT — Devdutt Pattanaik's reading (My Gita, 2015) and its critique

Every claim here carries `<!-- prov: FS-PATT -->`.

## The book
- **My Gita**, Devdutt Pattanaik, **Rupa Publications, 2015** (India). ~250 pages, illustrated
  by the author in his characteristic line-drawing style.
- Pattanaik (b. **1970**), a medical doctor by training, is India's best-selling mythology
  author; his stated method is retelling through the **beliefs of people**, not philology.

## His stated approach (from the book's own front matter and interviews)
- **Thematic, not sequential**: he reorganizes the Gita's material into **18 themes** (the
  number 18 itself being his organizing conceit), instead of walking chapter by chapter.
- **"My truth" subjectivity**: the title's "My" is deliberate — he frames all readings as
  subjective (this is his stated Vedantic-cum-postmodern hermeneutic: truth as darshana,
  a view from where one stands).
- He foregrounds **Krishna as the empathetic divine friend**, the Gita as a conversation about
  **human potential**, with vocabulary from modern self-help (ownership, ecosystem, potential,
  the "institutional" vs "personal").
- He explicitly brackets the battlefield: less war-room, more counselling room.

## The critique (must be presented alongside — this book's neutrality rule)
**Nityanand Misra's review in Swarajya (2017)**, "My Gita: Hindered by His Truth" — a detailed
philological critique. Specific objections (as listed in that review; verify at source before
quoting verbatim):
1. **Etymological errors**: e.g., deriving **brahman** incorrectly ( Misra argues Pattanaik's
   folk-etymology "brah-man = expand-mind" is not the philological account).
2. **kṣetra as "social body"**: Pattanaik renders kṣetra (field, 13.2) as the *social* body —
   a free rendering with no textual basis, per Misra.
3. **"Para-atma" coinage**: Pattanaik uses "paratma/param-atma" loosely where the text's terms
   are paramātman/ātman with distinct grammatical and doctrinal weight.
4. **deva/asura redefinition**: presenting asuras as "those who lack empathy" etc. — modern
   moralizing layered onto a term whose Vedic/Puranic semantics are different, per Misra.
- Our book's stance: Pattanaik is presented as **one lens** — valuable for accessibility and
  thematic clarity, contested for philological rigor. The reader decides. NEVER editorialize
  either way beyond attributing the praise and the critique.

## Where Pattanaik sits for our reader
- He is the reader's likely entry point (user's stated familiarity), so the pattanaik page
  must: (a) state his method plainly, (b) show his 18-theme structure, (c) present the Misra
  critique faithfully, (d) hand back to the many-gitas frame: his subjectivity claim is itself
  a 20th-c. intellectual move with its own history.
- Contrast pairs available: Pattanaik's thematic Gita vs our sequential walk; "his truth" vs
  the text's own colophon claims (brahmavidyā, yogaśāstra); empathetic Krishna vs 11.32's
  all-destroying time.
