## The commentarial landscape
The Gita is one of the most-commented texts in world literature. **Sadāle's 1935 compilation
(Bhāvārthadīpikā-and-others edition, 7 volumes)** gathers **eleven full Sanskrit commentaries**
in one critical edition. Source: IGNCA introduction PDF (ignca.gov.in/sanskrit/
bhagavad_gita_introduction.pdf), which lists the commentators and dates.

## The major commentators (chronological)

### Shankara (Śaṅkara) — Advaita Vedanta
- Traditionally **788–820 CE** (dates disputed; scholarly estimates place him anywhere in the
  7th–8th centuries CE). His **Gītābhāṣya** is the earliest complete commentary that survives
  in wide circulation, and the fountainhead of the non-dualist reading.
- Core claim: the ultimate is **brahman, one without a second**; the world of multiplicity is
  brought on by **māyā / avidyā** (concealment/ignorance); Krishna's teaching ultimately points
  to knowledge (jñāna) of identity: "you are that" (the Upaniṣadic mahāvākya framework).
- On 2.47 and action: works purify the mind, but liberation comes through knowledge; the karma
  path is a stepping stone, not the summit.
Source: IGNCA intro; advaita-vedanta.org tradition pages. Dates: standard reference works.

### Ramanuja (Rāmānuja) — Viśiṣṭādvaita
- **1017–1137 CE** (traditional dates). Commentary: **Gītābhāṣya**.
- Core claim: the self is a **mode (prakāra) of brahman**, not identical to it; brahman is
  qualified by conscious selves and matter. Liberation is not identity but **eternal loving
  service**. The self never dissolves.
- Reads 18.66 ("abandoning all dharmas, take refuge in me alone") as the **carama-śloka**, the
  final word: refuge (śaraṇāgati / **prapatti**) is the supreme means. For Ramanuja the Gita's
  climax is surrender, and works become an expression of that surrender.
Source: IGNCA intro; Viśiṣṭādvaita tradition literature.

### Madhva (Madhva) — Dvaita
- **1238–1317 CE**. Commentary: **Gītātātparya (or Gītābhāṣya)**.
- Core claim: **five eternal real distinctions** (God / souls / matter / God-souls / soul-matter);
  the soul is God's dependent, never identical with him. The Gita's final teaching is devotion to
  a real, sovereign Vishnu, with the soul's eternal separate bliss.
Source: IGNCA intro; Dvaita tradition literature.

### Other classical names (for the "other-classical" page)
- **Abhinavagupta** (c. **950–1016 CE**, Kashmir): **Gītārtha-saṅgraha** — a Shaiva reading; the
  Gita as teaching the identity of Shiva/Krishna as the self of all. One of the few non-Vaishnava,
  non-Advaita classical commentaries.
- **Jñānadeva / Jnaneshwar** (**1275–1296 CE**, Maharashtra): **Jñāneshwarī** — Marathi verse
  commentary; bhakti-saturated vernacular rendering that made the Gita a people's text in
  Maharashtra; foundational for later Marathi devotional culture (and, centuries later, quoted
  alongside Tilak's Gita Rahasya).
- **Yāmunācārya** (**916–1041 CE**): **Gītārtha-saṅgraha** — earliest Viśiṣṭādvaita summary; uses
  the three-hexad division (karma/bhakti/jñāna hexads) later echoed by Madhusudana.
- **Sridhara Swami** (early **15th c.**, Orissa): **Subodhinī** — the standard Advaita commentary
  for several centuries, the baseline many later writers cite or attack.
- **Vallabha** (**1479–1531 CE**): **Jñānadīpa / Gītārthadīpa** — Śuddhādvaita (pure non-dualism)
  reading; Krishna's grace and sweetness (the Pushti Marg devotional frame).
- **Madhusudana Sarasvati** (latter half of **16th c.**, Benares): **Gudhārtha-dīpikā** — "the
  lamp that reveals the hidden meaning"; Advaitin who nevertheless gives bhakti an honored
  middle hexad; famous for systematizing the karma/bhakti/jñāna 6+6+6 division.
- **Viśvanātha Chakravartin** (late **17th c.**, Bengal): **Sārārtha-varṣiṇī** — Gaudiya Vaishnava
  commentary; the Gita as pure devotional theism, in the line of Chaitanya.
- **Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa** (**18th c.**): **Gītābhūṣaṇa** — Gaudiya Vedanta (after Chaitanya);
  wrote it, tradition says, in response to Jaipur court pressure to produce a Vaishnava
  commentary on the prasthāna-traya.
Source for all of the above: IGNCA introduction PDF listing of commentators with dates;
cross-checked against standard Dvaita/Advaita/Viśiṣṭādvaita reference pages.

## The three-prasthāna frame
Classical Vedanta treats the Gita as one of three "starting points" (prasthāna-traya) with the
Upaniṣads and the Brahma Sutras. Every major Vedāntin had to comment on all three — which is
why the Gita accumulated commentaries from every school as a membership requirement, not merely
out of affection. Source: standard Vedanta intro (IGNCA; advaita-vedanta.org).

|---|---|---|---|
| 2.47 | works purify; knowledge frees | disciplined works as worship | works as devotion's body |
| 4.13 | varna by quality, ultimately unreal in brahman | real order, vocational | real, divinely grounded |
| 9.32 | all souls equally brahman | the lowliest soul can rise by grace | souls differ eternally |
| 13.2 | field and knower ultimately one | field is brahman's body | eternal distinction of the two |
| 18.66 | refuge = knowledge of identity | refuge = prapatti, self-surrender | refuge = devotion to a sovereign Lord |
